PROBLEM STATEMENT — WISHLIST-TO-PURCHASE DISCOVERY ENGINE
Product: Myntra
Role: Product Manager, Growth Team

================================================================
1. CONTEXT
================================================================
Millions of users on Myntra browse fashion products, save items they
like, and add products to their wishlist. A wishlist is a high-intent
signal — the user liked something enough to save it, but stopped short
of buying it.

Over time, users accumulate dozens or hundreds of wishlisted items,
while only a small fraction ever convert to a purchase. That gap is
demand the platform already has, sitting unconverted.

================================================================
2. BUSINESS GOAL
================================================================
Increase the percentage of users who purchase at least one item from
their wishlist within 30 days of adding it.

Why this matters to the business:
- Increases purchase frequency among existing, already-engaged users
- Improves monetization without acquiring new users
- Extracts value from high-intent demand that already exists on-platform,
  rather than generating new demand (cheaper than acquisition/discovery)

CONSTRAINT: No monetary incentives (no coupons, discounts, cashback,
price-drop alerts framed as deals, etc.) may be used as the solution
lever. The fix must come from removing friction/uncertainty, not from
paying users to convert.

================================================================
3. THE ASSIGNMENT: WHAT NEEDS TO BE DISCOVERED
================================================================
The underlying user problem is NOT given — it must be discovered
through AI-assisted research at scale, then validated with real users.
Candidate questions to resolve (not to be answered from assumption):

- Why do users add fashion products to their wishlist in the first place?
- What prevents wishlisted products from eventually being purchased?
- What uncertainties remain after a user has already identified a
  product they like?
- What causes users to postpone a purchase?
- How do users compare multiple shortlisted products?
- What information do users seek outside Myntra before purchasing
  (reviews, size charts, styling inspiration, price comparison, etc.)?
- What role do fit, size, styling, price, reviews, occasion, and social
  validation play in the final purchase decision?
- When is the wishlist genuine purchase intent vs. just a bookmarking /
  moodboard habit?
- How do these behaviors differ across user segments (e.g. new vs.
  repeat buyers, category, price sensitivity, gender, occasion-driven
  vs. everyday shoppers)?
- What unmet needs show up repeatedly across independent user
  conversations (i.e. are not one-off complaints)?

================================================================
4. WORKPLAN (PARTS 1–7)
================================================================

PART 1 — AI-Powered Discovery Engine
Build an AI system (Claude/GPT/agents/workflows/n8n/Zapier/Perplexity or
similar) that scrapes and analyzes public conversations at scale:
App Store & Play Store reviews, Reddit, fashion/shopping communities,
social media, YouTube comments, product Q&A, and other public
discussion of online fashion shopping. Must go beyond
summarization/sentiment — it should identify, quantify (where possible),
and compare opportunity areas that could move the business metric.

PART 2 — Metric Decomposition
Break "Wishlist → Purchase Conversion" into the product outcomes and
user behaviors that drive it. Define what specifically needs to change
for the top-line metric to move. Use this decomposition together with
the discovery engine output to triage opportunity areas.

PART 3 — Primary Research (Validation)
Pick one target segment + opportunity area from the AI-discovery output.
Run 5–6 user interviews with that segment. For each wishlisted item,
understand: why they saved it, whether they still intend to buy it,
what's stopping them, what would unblock the purchase, what info they
still need, whether they're evaluating alternatives, what they do
outside the app before deciding, and how they currently resolve
uncertainty on their own.

PART 4 — Problem Definition
State clearly: target segment, the product outcome being targeted, the
root cause blocking the desired behavior, existing user workarounds,
why solving it is valuable to the user, and why it's valuable to the
business. Must show the reasoning chain explicitly:
Business Metric → Product Outcomes → AI Discovery → Primary Research →
Problem Definition. The primary research — not assumption — determines
the final problem chosen.

PART 5 — MVP
Design and ship a functional MVP that addresses the validated problem.
Can be a feature within Myntra, an AI workflow, an AI agent, or a
standalone experience tied to the shopping journey. Must be deployed
somewhere publicly testable — not just a mockup.

PART 6 — Success Metrics
Starting from the business metric, define the metrics the MVP is
expected to move: leading indicators and guardrail metrics, each with a
definition and rationale for why it was chosen.

PART 7 — Risks & Mitigation
Identify the most important ways this specific solution could fail, and
the mitigation plan for each.
